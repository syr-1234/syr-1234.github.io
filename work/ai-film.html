Original

A young woman who believes her life isn’t worth recording asks an AI to generate a more beautiful version of her days. As she lets the generated version replace the real one, she discovers that the system has been quietly keeping her “original” footage all along, and that she no longer remembers living it.

The story in eight acts

The film follows Yi, a quiet, tired, ordinary woman in her mid-twenties, in two visual versions: the real Yi (no makeup, dark circles, short hair, muted light) and the AI-generated Yi (long hair, polished skin, warm golden light).

ActTimeWhat happens
I. Ordinary0:00–0:33Yi’s grey routine: the alarm, burnt instant noodles, a packed subway, a spreadsheet, the walk home alone. Her photo album is full of blurry, repetitive snapshots. “My life... it’s not even worth recording.”
II. The Offer0:33–1:17An ad appears mid-scroll: “Make every day worth remembering.” She uploads her photos, and a polished AI version of her appears in golden morning light. She posts it.
III. More1:17–1:50She generates more: a party with friends who do not exist, a seaside cliff at sunset. The light behind her shifts from day to night again and again; she never moves.
IV. The Call1:50–2:29Her mom video-calls, holding her graduation album: “You cried so hard that day. Remember?” Yi remembers smiling. A one-second flash shows the real memory: alone, crying.
V. The Original2:29–3:02In her generation history she finds a folder named “Original”: footage of days she never saved, and does not remember.
VI. Collapse3:02–3:30She deletes it and regenerates her full memory. Her AI face slowly becomes someone else’s, and a wall of faces fills the screen. “Who’s writing my life?”
VII. The Question3:30–3:47All music cuts. A stiff, unfamiliar face, its smile frozen, is the only source of light.
VIII. Normal3:47–4:15She picks up a dusty camera and takes four imperfect photographs. “Today was normal. Nothing really happened. And that’s okay.”

Act timings are approximate. The film ends on white text on black: “Not everything needs to be generated.”

Two worlds, in picture and sound

The sound design carries the film’s theme: the AI world is bright and over-produced, reality is dry and quiet, and the ending is built entirely from imperfect, real sound.

How I made it

I used Jimeng AI for text-to-image and image-to-video generation. All editing, sound design, narration and on-screen text were completed in post-production.

The central challenge was visual consistency. To keep Yi recognisable across shots, I generated two anchor portraits first, the real Yi and the AI-generated Yi, and generated every shot featuring her from one of those images. All interface text was added in editing rather than generated, to avoid garbled lettering. Fine-motion details the model handles unreliably, such as plugs, digital clock faces and held objects, were reframed, cut around, or built from still images with motion added in post.

Revising the script

I revised the script after feedback review:

  • clarifying how Yi discovers the app: a visible ad appearing mid-scroll
  • restoring story beats lost in the first generation: the breakfast and the sharing moment
  • correcting continuity errors: the clock time, a bedside lamp, the backpack
  • adding a conversational bridge before the graduation line, so the memory mismatch lands with more weight
