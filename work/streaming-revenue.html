Code and data

Who gets paid for a stream

For a data activism exercise, I put figures on musicians’ streaming royalties into a database and turned them into interactive charts, so that the economics of streaming would be easy for the public to see and discuss.

Course
COMM2754 Programming for Digital Media, University of Leeds
Year
2025
My role
Individual project
Tools
PHP, MySQL database in cPanel, Chart.js

Why this data

I chose musicians’ digital royalties because the numbers themselves make the strongest case for how little creators earn in the streaming era. Schrock (2016) describes civic hacking as data activism: requesting, digesting, modelling and contesting data. Turning a raw table into charts anyone can read is the digesting and modelling part.

How it works

I created a database in cPanel, wrote PHP to retrieve the data into two pages, a data table and a chart page, and used Chart.js to draw the charts.

What it shows

One figure made the point on its own: on Last.fm, a musician needs more than 15 million plays to earn the equivalent of a minimum wage. [待补充：数据来源是什么？]

Looking back

As media students we can do more than build websites. Obtaining, analysing and questioning data are ways to take on social responsibility, and code can bring the hidden workings of abstract systems to light.
