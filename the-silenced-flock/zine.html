A visual journal about women’s health posts silenced by algorithms

Why is it called the "Silent Flock"?

"Sheep" are often seen as a submissive, controllable, and easily driven group in cultural imagery. In our context, the “herd” refers to those posts, content, and voices discussing women’s health topics which are controlled, restricted, or even deleted by platform algorithms.

At the same time, the shape of the ovaries bears a striking visual resemblance to a sheep's head (see the "topic" section of the website for details). This biological coincidence makes "sheep" a symbol that is not random but has a metaphorical meaning for the body.

And "silence" is not because there is nothing to say, but because the words are hidden.

As we show in the "cases" section of the website:

  • Over 90% of survey participants have seen women’s health content removed or hidden (n=59, 2026);
  • Vaginal health (78%) and sex education (76%) are the two most restricted topics;
  • On platforms such as Xiaohongshu, words such as "PCOS" and "vagina" are often abbreviated or replaced, while men’s health content is not subject to the same restrictions.
We are not comparing women to animals

This must be made clear. We’re not saying “women are like sheep.” Our metaphor is: posts about women’s health are controlled, excluded, and silenced like sheep driven by algorithms.

The "sheep breaking free" movement on the cover and inside pages of the magazine is not about submission, but about resistance. They try to break out of the frame — just like many female users who still insist on sharing after being restricted (such as the blogger in the case who was restricted three times but continued to share her PCOS pregnancy experience).

Why make a magazine instead of just one more post?

Because we realize that topics restricted by algorithms often need to be spread through another medium.

The form of paper or digital zine is naturally artistic, storable, and algorithmic. It does not rely on recommendation systems, is not filtered by keywords, and does not rely on the number of likes to survive.

We hope to use this quieter, more powerful, and less hijacked way by algorithms to let more people see:

Women's health information is not "sensitive content" but "basic health rights."
